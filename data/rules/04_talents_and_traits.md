# Talents & Traits

Classes are gone. In their place stand two distinct unlock tracks: Talents, the power-progression axis, and Traits, the characterization axis with built-in trade-offs. You are not an archetype walking a rail. You are the sum of what you have chosen to master and who you have chosen to be.

## Talents

A Talent is a learned capability — the feat-analog. Talents are always beneficial: a physical or practiced improvement to the character, oriented toward combat and capability, though some reach into non-combat situations.

- **When you gain them:** one Talent at each of Levels 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10.
- **Gates:** every Talent requires a **minimum character level**. Some additionally require an **Attribute Die threshold** (for example, "Vigor d8+"). Some require other Talents first — these prerequisite chains are how the old subclass Paths survive, as 3-node Talent chains.
- **Costs:** a Talent's entry states what its abilities cost to use — Momentum, Reserve points, AP, or nothing at all. Talents never change what an action costs in AP unless their text says so explicitly.

### The Web Is Fully Open

Any Talent whose prerequisites you meet is available to you, regardless of what else you have taken. There is no archetype anchor, no locked list, no "your kind can't learn that." If you have the level, the die, and the prerequisites, the Talent is yours to take.

### Paths Are Guidance, Not Rules

Suggested paths — pre-built Talent sequences reproducing the eleven old archetype fantasies (Arcanist, Assassin, Berserker, Charger, Harrower, Invoker, Muse, Shaman, Stalker, Vanguard, Zealot) — ship with the game as guidance only. Following one to the letter, sampling from three, or ignoring them entirely are all equally legitimate. No rule ever references your path.

## Traits

A Trait is a definer — the background-analog. Traits describe personality, social bearing, and physical nature, and they can cut either way: a strength, or a strength with a paired weakness. Quick-witted or dim-witted. Perceptive or near-sighted. Agile or hobble-footed. Some Traits reach into combat, but their home ground is who your character is when the swords are down.

- **When you gain them:** you **choose** your Traits at character creation — 2 of them `(tunable)` — and choose an additional Trait at Levels 5 and 10 `(tunable)`. Traits are never assigned or rolled; the paired weakness is a price you pick with open eyes.
- **Gates:** every Trait requires an **Attribute Die threshold**. Traits have no level requirement — the die is the gate.
- **Flavors:** Traits come in two flavors: **Social** (bearing, presence, connections, manner) and **Good/Bad** (paired strength-and-weakness definers).

## Talents vs. Traits at a Glance

| | **Talents** | **Traits** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gate | Minimum character level (sometimes + Attribute Die, + prerequisites) | Attribute Die threshold |
| Valence | Always positive | Can be a strength *or* a paired strength/weakness |
| Focus | Combat / capability | Personality / social / physical definers |
| Analogy | Feat | Background feature |
| Cadence | Levels 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 | Creation, plus Levels 5 and 10 `(tunable)` |

The full Talent and Trait lists are data files (`data/talents.json`, `data/traits.json`), written in Phase 2.
