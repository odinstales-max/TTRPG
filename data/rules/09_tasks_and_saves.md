# Tasks & Saves

## Challenge Threshold

When a character attempts a task with a meaningful chance of failure, the GM sets a Challenge Threshold. The player rolls the Attribute Die most relevant to the task. If the result meets or exceeds the Threshold, they succeed.

| **Difficulty** | **Target Number** | **Representative Task** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | 3 | Something trained individuals can do under pressure |
| Challenging | 5 | A test that demands skill and focus |
| Demanding | 7 | The limit of what most people can reliably achieve |
| Extreme | 9 | Feats that define legendary careers |

## Tasks

A Task is any meaningful attempt to accomplish something with uncertain outcomes outside of direct combat. Picking a lock, tracking prey across a shifting marsh, bartering with a suspicious merchant, scaling a rain-slicked cliff in the dark. When the outcome matters and failure is possible, the GM calls for a Task roll. A Task roll uses the same mechanic as any other roll: choose the Attribute Die most relevant to the attempt and roll it against the Challenge Threshold the GM has set. Meet or exceed the Threshold and the Task succeeds.

Not every action requires a Task roll. The GM should only call for one when two conditions are both true: the outcome is genuinely uncertain, and failure produces an interesting consequence. A trained physician stabilizing a stable patient does not need a roll. A physician operating in a flooded ruin by candlelight while a hostile patrol sweeps the floor above does.

### Choosing the Attribute

The GM names the Attribute before the roll is made, but players may propose an alternative if they can justify it. Prying open a sealed vault door is Vigor by default, but a character who understands the mechanism might reasonably argue for Focus. The GM has final say. The four Attributes cover the full range of Task approaches:

- **Vigor:** Physical feats of strength, endurance, and raw force. Forcing open a sealed door, swimming against a current, carrying a wounded ally across a battlefield, resisting a disease taking hold in the body.

- **Agility:** Speed, precision, and physical dexterity. Picking a lock, moving silently through a crowded hall, scaling a cliff face, threading a needle in the dark, catching a thrown object mid-air.

- **Focus:** Intellect, perception, and arcane discipline. Deciphering a cipher, identifying a poison by its smell, navigating by dead reckoning, recalling obscure lore, tracking through difficult terrain by reading environmental signs.

- **Resolve:** Conviction, personality, and psychological strength. Convincing a reluctant informant, maintaining composure while interrogated, inspiring a demoralized group, holding position against overwhelming dread.

### Degrees of Success and Failure

Task rolls do not produce a flat success or failure. The margin matters. The GM interprets results using the following guidance as a baseline:

- **Clean Success (meets or exceeds the Threshold):** The character accomplishes exactly what they attempted. No complications, no cost.

- **Failure by 1 or 2 (near miss):** The character falls short, but the situation does not dramatically worsen. They may try again if circumstances permit, or simply find another path. The rolling character gains 1 Momentum Token.

- **Failure by 3 or more (hard failure):** The character fails and the situation changes in a meaningful way. An alarm is triggered, a contact grows hostile, a rope frays. The GM introduces a complication. The rolling character gains 2 Momentum Tokens.

### Skills

Skills are a fixed list of sixteen, each keyed to an Attribute, and training in them is
binary: you are trained or you are not. When a Task falls squarely inside a Skill you have,
**Upgrade your Attribute Die for that roll.**

| Attribute | Skills |
| --- | --- |
| Vigor | Athletics, Smithing |
| Agility | Stealth, Lockwork, Sleight of Hand, Acrobatics |
| Focus | Scavenging, Ruin-Lore, Field Medicine, Toxicology, Survival, Arcana |
| Resolve | Persuasion, Deception, Command, Beast-Handling |

Two limits keep Skills honest. A Skill applies only to Tasks — never to an Impact Roll and
never to a Save. And the GM has final say on whether a Skill covers the attempt at hand: the
list describes training, not a claim on the world. Skills come from your Origin, Trade, and
Turn at creation; see [Character Creation](02_character_creation.md).

### Spending Momentum on Tasks

Momentum is not locked to combat. Before any Task roll, you may spend your own Momentum Tokens — on your roll, or on a roll made by an ally within your immediate engagement — each token adding +1 to the result. A character about to attempt a vital negotiation can benefit from accumulated tension just as much as a warrior can on the battlefield. Remember that all pools reset when combat begins; momentum carried out of a tense scene lasts only until the next fight starts.

### Group Tasks

When the entire party is attempting the same Task together, such as crossing a treacherous passage or moving silently past a patrol, the GM may call for a Group Task. Each player rolls the relevant Attribute Die. Count the results. If more than half of the rolls meet or exceed the Challenge Threshold, the group succeeds. If half or fewer succeed, the group fails and the consequence applies to everyone. Characters who failed individually may still contribute to a group success; outcomes reflect collective performance, not individual excellence.

### Assisting

A character who is not the primary actor may attempt to assist. They describe how they are helping, roll the relevant Attribute Die, and if their result meets a Routine Threshold (3), the primary actor rolls their Task die with it Upgraded by one step. Only one character may assist on any given Task. Assistance is not always possible: the GM determines whether the nature of the Task permits it. Some things can only be done alone.

## Saves

A Save is a reactive roll made when something is happening to your character rather than something your character is initiating. When a trap springs, a spell compels you, poison takes hold, or fear claws at your mind, the GM calls for a Save. You roll the Attribute Die most relevant to what you are resisting and compare it to the Threshold set by the triggering ability or condition.

The mechanic is identical to a Task roll: meet or exceed the Threshold and you succeed. Fall short and the effect applies. The distinction between a Save and a Task is intentional and meaningful. Abilities, spells, Talents, and conditions may affect one without affecting the other. Something that grants an Upgrade on Saves does not grant an Upgrade on Tasks, and vice versa, unless it explicitly states otherwise.

The four Attributes cover the full range of things a character might need to resist. Vigor Saves resist physical punishment, disease, and bodily harm. Agility Saves resist effects that require quick reactions or evasion. Focus Saves resist arcane compulsion, sensory disruption, and mental contamination. Resolve Saves resist fear, psychic assault, and attacks on the will.

A character's own failed Save does not generate Momentum for them. A Save represents resisting harm, not attempting something bold, so falling to a hostile effect is simply the effect landing. There is one important counterpart to this rule, however. When a target succeeds on a Save against a hostile spell or ability that you cast, you gain 1 Momentum Token. Your committed effort was resisted rather than wasted, and the system reflects that the same way it reflects a Glancing Blow: the attempt still feeds your momentum. In short, the creature making the Save never gains Momentum from it, but the caster whose effect was resisted does.
