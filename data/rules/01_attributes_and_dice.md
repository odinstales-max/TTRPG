# Attributes & Dice

## The Attributes

Every character in [GAME NAME] is defined by four core Attributes. These Attributes are not static numbers, they are dice. Specifically, they are measured on a Step-Die scale, representing a character's capability from barely competent to the absolute peak of human potential: d4 (Weak), d6 (Standard), d8 (Skilled), d10 (Expert), and d12 (Legendary). When you roll an Attribute, you roll that die. When you improve an Attribute, you replace the die with the next step up the scale.

- **Vigor:** Physical power, endurance, and raw strength. Governs melee strikes, feats of athletics, and pushing the body to its absolute physical limits. Your Vigor Die determines your Vigor Defense rating.

- **Agility:** Speed, reflexes, and precision. Governs ranged attacks, acrobatic maneuvers, and evading immediate danger. Your Agility Die determines your Agility Defense rating.

- **Focus:** Intellect, perception, and arcane discipline. Governs spellcasting, environmental investigation, and solving complex problems. Your Focus Die determines your Focus Defense rating.

- **Resolve:** Conviction, ego, and psychological fortitude. Governs commanding allies, resisting fear, and surviving the psychological horrors of the world. Your Resolve Die determines your Resolve Defense rating.

## The Four Defense Ratings

Every character possesses four Defense Ratings, one for each Attribute. Defense Ratings are the barrier between an attacker's impact and your Hit Points. When you are struck, the attacker rolls their Impact Pool and subtracts the appropriate Defense Rating from the total. Whatever remains is the damage you take.

Defense Ratings begin at a baseline derived from your Attribute Dice and are then modified upward by Talents, armor, and equipment. The baseline formula is:

> **BASELINE DEFENSE FORMULA**
> Baseline Defense Rating = Half the maximum face value of the governing Attribute Die.
> d4 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 2
> d6 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 3
> d8 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 4
> d10 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 5
> d12 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 6
> As your Attribute Die improves through leveling, its corresponding Defense Rating improves automatically. Offense and defense scale through the same mechanic.

| **Defense Rating** | **Attribute** | **Absorbs** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vigor Defense | Vigor | Melee strikes, Slashing, Piercing, Bludgeoning |
| Agility Defense | Agility | Ranged attacks, Fire, Frost, Volt, Force, Thunder, Acid |
| Focus Defense | Focus | Arcane spells, Void, Poison, direct magical attacks |
| Resolve Defense | Resolve | Psychic attacks, Radiant, mental and spiritual assault |

When an attacker makes an Impact Roll, the ability or weapon used will specify which Defense Rating it targets. A sword targets Vigor Defense. A fire spell targets Agility Defense. A necrotic bolt targets Focus Defense. A psychic lance targets Resolve Defense. If a Defense Rating is reduced to 0, it cannot be reduced further, a rating of 0 means all impact of that category passes through as direct damage.

## True Damage

Some abilities, conditions, and effects deal True Damage. True Damage bypasses all four Defense Ratings entirely and is applied directly as a reduction to Hit Points. It cannot be mitigated by armor, features that reduce incoming damage, or any modifier that references a specific Defense type. True Damage represents harm that strikes at a fundamental level, internal rupture, soul erosion, or catastrophic systemic failure.

## Visceral Attacks

Some high-tier spells and abilities are designated as Visceral. A Visceral attack rolls against a specific Defense Rating to determine whether the attack connects, but if it hits, all damage is dealt as True Damage, bypassing every Defense Rating including the one rolled against. The Defense Rating roll determines success or failure; True Damage determines the result of that success. Visceral attacks operate at a level beyond ordinary physical or magical resistance: strikes that target the body's fundamental structure rather than its surface defenses. On a Glancing Blow, a Visceral attack deals no damage and produces whatever Glancing Blow effect is stated in the ability description, including the standard Momentum Token for the attacker. Effects that Downgrade the attacker's dice work normally against Visceral attacks, shrinking the roll before it meets the Defense Rating; effects that reduce incoming damage after a hit do not, because the damage arrives as True Damage. As a system-wide convention, every effect that bypasses a Defense Rating is written in one of two ways: damage that is already determined is "dealt as True Damage," while an attack that must still roll to connect before bypassing is designated Visceral. Every effect that lowers a Defense Rating is written as Sunder. No other phrasing grants armor bypass or reduction.

## Damage Types

All damage dealt in [GAME NAME] belongs to one of thirteen damage types, organized into seven categories. The damage type determines which Defense Rating absorbs it, and informs which enemies resist or are vulnerable to a given attack. Resistances and immunities are meaningful and prevalent throughout the world, choosing the right damage type for the right enemy is a genuine tactical decision.

### Physical

| **Type** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| Slashing | Bladed weapons, claws, and cutting edges. Targets Vigor Defense. |
| Piercing | Thrusting weapons, arrows, fangs, and spines. Targets Vigor Defense. |
| Bludgeoning | Blunt force, falls, and crushing weight. Targets Vigor Defense. |

### Elemental

| **Type** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| Fire | Heat and flame energy. Targets Agility Defense. |
| Frost | Cold and ice. Targets Agility Defense. |
| Volt | Lightning and electrical energy. Targets Agility Defense. |
| Force | Pure magical kinetic energy. Targets Agility Defense. Force damage inherently ignores all Cover bonuses, physical barriers do not impede it. |
| Thunder | Shockwaves, pressure, and the violence of sound weaponized. Targets Agility Defense. |
| Acid | Corrosive chemical harm that dissolves armor and flesh. Targets Agility Defense. |

### Shadow, Mental & Divine

| **Type** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| Void | Entropy, decay, and soul erosion. Targets Focus Defense. |
| Poison | Biological toxins and venoms. Targets Focus Defense. |
| Psychic | Direct mental assault targeting cognition, memory, and perception. Targets Resolve Defense. |
| Radiant | The energy of absolute conviction and cosmic authority. Targets Resolve Defense. |

Note: Cover bonuses apply only to Vigor Defense and Agility Defense. A wall stops a sword and deflects a fireball. It does not stop a psychic lance or a void bolt, attacks targeting Focus Defense or Resolve Defense ignore all cover modifiers.

## Upgrading and Downgrading Dice

The primary way [GAME NAME] handles difficulty and power scaling is through the Step-Die scale. Upgrading a die shifts it one step to the right (d6 becomes d8). Downgrading shifts it one step to the left. A die cannot be Upgraded beyond d12 or Downgraded below d4. Multiple Upgrades and Downgrades offset each other before resolving.

Step-Die Ceiling Overflow: If a die is already at d12 and would be Upgraded further, each excess Upgrade beyond d12 instead adds an overflow die: roll a d4 alongside the d12 and add its result. Each further Upgrade steps the overflow die up the ladder instead of adding another die (a d12 with two Upgrades rolls a d12 plus a d6; with three, a d12 plus a d8). The overflow die is a true die: it can Explode, and it benefits from properties such as Vicious exactly as the main die does. The overflow die applies only to the triggering roll and does not permanently alter the die.

## Upgrading and Downgrading Rolls

When a roll is Upgraded, shift the Attribute Die one step up the scale before rolling. When a roll is Downgraded, shift it one step down. Multiple Upgrades and Downgrades on the same roll offset each other before resolving. A die cannot be Upgraded beyond d12 or Downgraded below d4 through this mechanism; overflow rules apply as described above.

These temporary Upgrades act on the roll, not the die itself. A permanent Attribute Die of d10 pushed to d12 by a roll Upgrade is still a d10 Attribute — see [Progression](03_progression.md) for the permanent d10 cap and the road to a true d12.
