# Spellcasting

## Schools of Magic

All magic in [GAME NAME] is organized into eight Schools. Magic is not forbidden or mysterious in its origins, it is a discipline, studied and refined through practice and scholarship. Colleges, traditions, and orders form around Schools, and a spellcaster's identity is often shaped by which Schools they have mastered. **Spell access is granted by Talents, per School** — a School-access Talent opens that School's list to you. No character is born a caster; casters are built, one School at a time.

| **School** | **Domain** | **Primary Damage Types** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evocation | Raw magical energy, direct damage, and elemental shaping. | Fire, Frost, Volt, Force, Thunder |
| Abjuration | Warding, protection, negation of hostile magic, and defensive barriers. | Radiant, Force |
| Conjuration | Summoning entities, creating matter from nothing, and teleportation. | Varies by conjured form |
| Transmutation | Altering the physical and chemical properties of matter and living beings. | Acid, Poison, Bludgeoning |
| Divination | Information gathering, sensing, battlefield awareness, and foresight. | None (utility) |
| Illusion | Misdirection, manufactured perception, and psychological deception. | Psychic |
| Entropy | Void magic, decay, the unraveling of living systems, and soul erosion. | Void, Poison |
| Resonance | Psychic and emotional magic, mind alteration, and force of personality. | Psychic, Radiant |

Casting a spell costs **1 AP** (Spellcasting category) plus the spell's Reserve cost. Spells tagged as Reactions use your Reaction instead; spells tagged as Free effects cost neither.

## Spell Tiers

All spells are organized into four tiers of power. Tier determines both the number of Impact Dice a spell rolls and the range of its Reserve cost. As a caster's Attribute Die improves through leveling, the size of those dice grows, meaning Tier and Attribute Die scale independently, rewarding characters who invest deeply in both.

| **Tier** | **Reserve Cost** | **Impact Dice Rolled** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 0 | 0 | 1 Attribute Die | Free baseline spells. You have access to the Tier 0 spells of every School you have unlocked, at all times. |
| Tier 1 | 1–2 Focus | 1 Attribute Die | Reliable, frequently cast spells. The cost buys better effects, conditions, or range over Tier 0. |
| Tier 2 | 3–4 Focus | 2 Attribute Dice | Significant spells with major battlefield impact. |
| Tier 3 | 5–6 Focus | 3 Attribute Dice | The most powerful spells available. Cast sparingly and decisively. |

Within each tier's cost range, the caster chooses how much to spend. Spending the higher amount within a tier unlocks an enhanced version of the spell's effect, as described in each spell's entry. A caster is never forced to overspend, casting a Tier 2 spell at its minimum cost of 3 is always valid.

## Scaling Spells

Many spells are written to grow when cast at a higher Tier than the one at which they were learned. A spell with this property notes the increase in its entry, usually as a line beginning "at higher Tiers." This is how a single spell remains useful across a career instead of being replaced by a near-identical version with a longer range.

A caster may cast any spell they know at any Tier from the spell's base Tier up to the highest Tier they have unlocked, provided they can pay that Tier's Reserve cost. Casting a spell at a higher Tier uses that Tier's Reserve cost and rolls that Tier's number of Impact Dice, and it unlocks the scaled values the spell specifies: greater range, a wider radius, a longer duration, additional targets, or a higher Save Difficulty. The spell's identity never changes when scaled. A bolt is still a bolt; it simply reaches farther and strikes harder. A spell only ever gains the specific increases written in its entry. If a spell lists no scaling, it functions identically at every Tier and is cast at its base Tier cost.

Save Difficulties rise modestly as a spell is cast at higher Tiers, so that higher-Tier effects land more reliably against the same target. Unless a spell states its own Difficulty, use the standard progression: Difficulty 5 at Tiers 0 and 1, Difficulty 7 at Tier 2, and Difficulty 9 at Tier 3.

## Sustained Spells

Some spells can be maintained beyond their normal duration. When a spell has the Sustained tag, the following rules apply.

### Maintaining a Sustained Spell

At the start of each of your turns you must spend 1 Focus Point to maintain each active Sustained spell. If you cannot or choose not to pay this cost the spell ends immediately.

You may maintain multiple Sustained spells simultaneously. The Focus cost applies to each independently.

### Concentration Saves

When you take damage while maintaining one or more Sustained spells, make a separate Concentration Save for each one: roll your Focus Die against a Difficulty of 5 plus twice that spell's tier. On a failure that spell ends immediately. On a success the spell holds.

## Channeled Spells

A handful of spells carry the Channeled tag instead of Sustained. A Channeled spell is one you actively drive with your own effort across multiple turns; it exists only for as long as you keep pouring power into it. Channeled is distinct from Sustained: a Sustained spell lingers on its own once cast and asks only for upkeep, while a Channeled spell collapses the instant your commitment to it lapses.

### Driving a Channel

Maintaining a Channeled spell costs **1 AP** on each of your turns — but the commitment is total: if you spend AP on any other action that turn, the channel breaks and the spell ends `(tunable)`. You make a Concentration Save whenever you take damage while channeling, exactly as a Sustained spell requires: roll your Focus Die against a Difficulty of 5 plus twice the spell's tier, and on a failure the channel breaks and the spell ends. The channel also breaks immediately, ending the spell, if you become Incapacitated or are Silenced while channeling a Verbal spell. Because a Channeled spell consumes your entire turn and ends the moment that commitment is interrupted, it is the only context in which the game's most extreme effects, such as Sunder that can stack a Defense Rating all the way to 0, are permitted.

## Spellcasting Components

Component types describe how a spell is performed. They are primarily flavor and impose no hand requirements: a caster with empty hands can cast any spell they know. Components matter in only two situations, described below.

**Verbal.** The spell requires speech. A character who is Silenced, gagged, or otherwise unable to produce sound cannot cast Verbal spells. This is the one hard component restriction.

**Somatic.** The spell requires gesture. Somatic spells can be cast with a free hand, while holding an Arcane Armament, or while holding a weapon, since a caster's movements are not so precise as to be blocked by a full hand. Somatic components impose no mechanical cost on their own.

**Material.** The spell consumes or focuses through physical matter. A Material spell requires that you either hold a spellcasting focus or an Arcane Armament, or have the specific material a spell names. A caster with truly empty hands and no focus cannot cast Material spells; any Armament satisfies this requirement.

## Arcane Armaments

An Arcane Armament is a caster's equivalent of a weapon: an implement that channels magic and grants an Explosion Rider to the spells cast through it. Just as a weapon's Rider fires when a martial character's Impact die explodes, an Armament's Rider fires when a spell's Impact die explodes (rolls its maximum face). A spell cast with no Armament in hand still functions normally; it simply gains no Rider. Casting a Glancing Blow never triggers a Rider, only an Explosion does.

Armaments carry the Conduit tag, which is the property that allows a spell's exploding die to inherit the Armament's Rider. An implement without the Conduit tag is a cosmetic focus only. As with weapons, if a character casts through an Armament they do not have access to (see [Equipment — Weapon and Armament Access](12_equipment.md)), the Armament's Rider does not apply.

Armaments are balanced as peers. None is mechanically stronger for occupying more hands, and no Armament adds a die to your Impact Pool; the bonus die remains exclusive to Heavy weapons. Whether an Armament is worn or held is a matter of logistics (a held Armament occupies a hand that could otherwise carry a weapon or shield), not of power. A caster chooses an Armament for the Rider that fits their style, not for a hand-count advantage. The arcane Staff described here is a distinct object from the martial Quarterstaff in the weapon tables; one channels spells, the other is swung.

The standard Arcane Armaments and their Explosion Riders:

- **Wand** (held, one hand). Rider, Pierce: the bonus damage from the exploding die is dealt as True Damage.

- **Rod** (held, one hand). Rider, Punish: the Defense Rating this spell targeted is Sundered by 1 until the end of your next turn.

- **Orb** (held or worn). Rider, Resonate: you generate 1 additional Momentum Token.

- **Relic** (worn). Rider, Echo: one other enemy within 10ft of the target takes damage of the spell's type equal to the Explosion die.

- **Holy Symbol** (worn). Rider, Sanctify: you or an ally within 30ft regains Hit Points equal to the Explosion die.

- **Totem** (held or worn). Rider, Spirit Surge: the next ally to act before your next turn Upgrades their first Impact Die.

- **Staff** (held, two hands). Rider, Cascade: the bonus Explosion die is Upgraded one step, and if it too rolls its maximum it explodes and Upgrades again.

- **Spell Book** (held). Rider, Compound: you may immediately apply one Tier 0 spell you know to the same target as a Free effect.

## Conjured Objects and Structures

Some spells and Talents conjure physical objects, barriers, walls, and structures into existence. These are real, tangible, and can be destroyed by enemies. Unless a spell specifies otherwise, all conjured objects follow these rules.

### Defense Ratings of Conjured Objects

Conjured objects have a Vigor Defense equal to half the maximum face value of the caster's current Focus Die, following the same baseline formula as character defenses. They have an Agility Defense of 0, elemental damage passes through conjured matter without reduction. They have a Focus Defense of 0 and a Resolve Defense of 0. Conjured objects cannot be targeted by effects that require a living creature.

### Hit Points and Destruction

Each conjured object has a specific HP value defined by the spell that created it. When a conjured object is reduced to 0 HP, it is instantly destroyed and vanishes completely unless the spell specifies that debris or residue remains. Area-of-effect spells and attacks that overlap with a conjured object deal their full damage to it. A single powerful strike from a Tier 2 or Tier 3 Evocation spell can bring down most Tier 1 conjured barriers in a single round, conjured terrain is tactically meaningful but not permanent or indestructible.

### Targeting Conjured Objects

Any creature may target a conjured object with an attack or spell, provided they have line of sight to it. Attacks targeting a conjured object always use the object's Vigor Defense as the relevant defense, regardless of the damage type of the attack.

## Illusion and Belief

Illusion spells create false sensory information that creatures experience as real. An illusion is believed until a creature has concrete cause to doubt it. The GM determines when a disbelief opportunity arises based on the following triggers.

### Disbelief Triggers

- **Physical Contact:** A creature physically passes through an illusion or an illusory entity.

- **Zero Damage:** A creature takes zero damage from an attack made by an illusory entity.

- **Active Scrutiny:** A creature spends 1 AP (Scrutinize) to actively examine the illusion and succeeds on a Focus Save where the Difficulty equals the caster's Focus Defense. This is not an opposed roll; the caster's Focus Defense serves as the Target Number.

### Effect of Disbelief

A creature that successfully disbelieves an illusion sees through it entirely. The illusion ceases to affect that creature's behavior. However, disbelief is a personal realization; it does not automatically communicate itself to allies. A creature that disbelieves may spend 1 AP to warn one ally, granting that ally an immediate scrutiny attempt without needing to spend their own AP.

### Sensory Range of Illusions

Not all illusions affect every sense equally. Tier 0 and Tier 1 Concealment and Fabrication spells affect sight only unless the spell explicitly states otherwise. Tier 2 and higher Illusion spells affect all senses including smell, sound, touch, and magical detection unless the spell explicitly states a limitation. Individual spells will note their sensory range in their effect text.

### Compelled Movement and Prone

Some Illusion spells compel a creature to move in a specific direction. If a creature subject to compelled movement is currently Prone, they must first spend 1 AP to stand before the compelled movement executes. If they choose not to stand or cannot do so, the compelled movement is lost but all other effects of the spell still apply normally. The Prone condition does not grant immunity to compulsion; it simply requires the standard cost to stand before movement can occur.

Phantasm spells are the only Illusion spells that deal damage. Their damage is Psychic and targets Resolve Defense, representing the psychological shock of experiencing a terrifying false reality. If a creature successfully disbelieves a Phantasm spell before its next damage tick, the ongoing damage stops immediately as the psychological mechanism that made the harm real is removed.
