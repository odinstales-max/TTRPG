**CHAPTER 2 - CORE RULES V3.2**

Era of Silence  │  System Reference

In [GAME NAME], survival is a calculation of risk, momentum, and raw capability. The world is dangerous, and the people who endure it are defined not by what they were given but by what they are willing to push themselves to become. These rules govern how you navigate the land, engage in lethal combat, and endure the hardships the world throws at you. Every mechanic in this chapter is designed around a single principle: your dice tell the story. There are few static modifiers. The die you carry is your capability, and pushing it to its limit is the most dangerous, most exhilarating thing you will ever do.

**THE ATTRIBUTES**

Every character in [GAME NAME] is defined by four core Attributes. These Attributes are not static numbers, they are dice. Specifically, they are measured on a Step-Die scale, representing a character's capability from barely competent to the absolute peak of human potential: d4 (Weak), d6 (Standard), d8 (Skilled), d10 (Expert), and d12 (Legendary). When you roll an Attribute, you roll that die. When you improve an Attribute, you replace the die with the next step up the scale.

- **Vigor:** Physical power, endurance, and raw strength. Governs melee strikes, feats of athletics, and pushing the body to its absolute physical limits. Your Vigor Die determines your Vigor Defense rating.

- **Agility:** Speed, reflexes, and precision. Governs ranged attacks, acrobatic maneuvers, and evading immediate danger. Your Agility Die determines your Agility Defense rating.

- **Focus:** Intellect, perception, and arcane discipline. Governs spellcasting, environmental investigation, and solving complex problems. Your Focus Die determines your Focus Defense rating.

- **Resolve:** Conviction, ego, and psychological fortitude. Governs commanding allies, resisting fear, and surviving the psychological horrors of the world. Your Resolve Die determines your Resolve Defense rating.

**THE FOUR DEFENSE RATINGS**

Every character possesses four Defense Ratings, one for each Attribute. Defense Ratings are the barrier between an attacker's impact and your Hit Points. When you are struck, the attacker rolls their Impact Pool and subtracts the appropriate Defense Rating from the total. Whatever remains is the damage you take.

Defense Ratings begin at a baseline derived from your Attribute Dice and are then modified upward by class features, armor, and equipment. The baseline formula is:

| **BASELINE DEFENSE FORMULA** Baseline Defense Rating = Half the maximum face value of the governing Attribute Die. d4 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 2 d6 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 3 d8 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 4 d10 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 5 d12 Attribute → Baseline Defense of 6 As your Attribute Die improves through leveling, its corresponding Defense Rating improves automatically. Offense and defense scale through the same mechanic. |
| --- |

| **Defense Rating** | **Attribute** | **Absorbs** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vigor Defense | Vigor | Melee strikes, Slashing, Piercing, Bludgeoning |
| Agility Defense | Agility | Ranged attacks, Fire, Frost, Volt, Force, Thunder, Acid |
| Focus Defense | Focus | Arcane spells, Void, Poison, direct magical attacks |
| Resolve Defense | Resolve | Psychic attacks, Radiant, mental and spiritual assault |

When an attacker makes an Impact Roll, the ability or weapon used will specify which Defense Rating it targets. A sword targets Vigor Defense. A fire spell targets Agility Defense. A necrotic bolt targets Focus Defense. A psychic lance targets Resolve Defense. If a Defense Rating is reduced to 0, it cannot be reduced further, a rating of 0 means all impact of that category passes through as direct damage.

**True Damage**

Some abilities, conditions, and effects deal True Damage. True Damage bypasses all four Defense Ratings entirely and is applied directly as a reduction to Hit Points. It cannot be mitigated by armor, class features that reduce incoming damage, or any modifier that references a specific Defense type. True Damage represents harm that strikes at a fundamental level, internal rupture, soul erosion, or catastrophic systemic failure.

**Visceral Attacks**

Some high-tier spells and abilities are designated as Visceral. A Visceral attack rolls against a specific Defense Rating to determine whether the attack connects, but if it hits, all damage is dealt as True Damage, bypassing every Defense Rating including the one rolled against. The Defense Rating roll determines success or failure; True Damage determines the result of that success. Visceral attacks operate at a level beyond ordinary physical or magical resistance: strikes that target the body's fundamental structure rather than its surface defenses. On a Glancing Blow, a Visceral attack deals no damage and produces whatever Glancing Blow effect is stated in the ability description, including the standard Momentum Token for the attacker. Effects that Downgrade the attacker's dice work normally against Visceral attacks, shrinking the roll before it meets the Defense Rating; effects that reduce incoming damage after a hit do not, because the damage arrives as True Damage. As a system-wide convention, every effect that bypasses a Defense Rating is written in one of two ways: damage that is already determined is “dealt as True Damage,” while an attack that must still roll to connect before bypassing is designated Visceral. Every effect that lowers a Defense Rating is written as Sunder. No other phrasing grants armor bypass or reduction.

**SPELL TIERS**

All spells are organized into four tiers of power. Tier determines both the number of Impact Dice a spell rolls and the range of its Reserve cost. As a caster's Attribute Die improves through leveling, the size of those dice grows, meaning Tier and Attribute Die scale independently, rewarding characters who invest deeply in both.

| **Tier** | **Reserve Cost** | **Impact Dice Rolled** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 0 | 0 | 1 Attribute Die | Free baseline spells. Every caster has access to their class Tier 0 spells at all times. |
| Tier 1 | 1–2 Focus | 1 Attribute Die | Reliable, frequently cast spells. The cost buys better effects, conditions, or range over Tier 0. |
| Tier 2 | 3–4 Focus | 2 Attribute Dice | Significant spells with major battlefield impact. |
| Tier 3 | 5–6 Focus | 3 Attribute Dice | The most powerful spells available. Cast sparingly and decisively. |

Within each tier's cost range, the caster chooses how much to spend. Spending the higher amount within a tier unlocks an enhanced version of the spell's effect, as described in each spell's entry. A caster is never forced to overspend, casting a Tier 2 spell at its minimum cost of 3 is always valid.

**SCALING SPELLS**

Many spells are written to grow when cast at a higher Tier than the one at which they were learned. A spell with this property notes the increase in its entry, usually as a line beginning "at higher Tiers." This is how a single spell remains useful across a career instead of being replaced by a near-identical version with a longer range.

A caster may cast any spell they know at any Tier from the spell's base Tier up to the highest Tier they have unlocked, provided they can pay that Tier's Reserve cost. Casting a spell at a higher Tier uses that Tier's Reserve cost and rolls that Tier's number of Impact Dice, and it unlocks the scaled values the spell specifies: greater range, a wider radius, a longer duration, additional targets, or a higher Save Difficulty. The spell's identity never changes when scaled. A bolt is still a bolt; it simply reaches farther and strikes harder. A spell only ever gains the specific increases written in its entry. If a spell lists no scaling, it functions identically at every Tier and is cast at its base Tier cost.

Save Difficulties rise modestly as a spell is cast at higher Tiers, so that higher-Tier effects land more reliably against the same target. Unless a spell states its own Difficulty, use the standard progression: Difficulty 5 at Tiers 0 and 1, Difficulty 7 at Tier 2, and Difficulty 9 at Tier 3.

**SUSTAINED SPELLS**

Some spells can be maintained beyond their normal duration. When a spell has the Sustained tag, the following rules apply.

**Maintaining a Sustained Spell**

At the start of each of your turns you must spend 1 Focus Point to maintain each active Sustained spell. If you cannot or choose not to pay this cost the spell ends immediately.

You may maintain multiple Sustained spells simultaneously. The Focus cost applies to each independently.

**Concentration Saves**

When you take damage while maintaining one or more Sustained spells, make a separate Concentration Save for each one: roll your Focus Die against a Difficulty of 5 plus twice that spell’s tier. On a failure that spell ends immediately. On a success the spell holds.

**CHANNELED SPELLS**

A handful of spells carry the Channeled tag instead of Sustained. A Channeled spell is one you actively drive with your own effort across multiple turns; it exists only for as long as you keep pouring power into it. Channeled is distinct from Sustained: a Sustained spell lingers on its own once cast and asks only for upkeep, while a Channeled spell collapses the instant your commitment to it lapses.

**Driving a Channel**

Maintaining a Channeled spell costs your Standard Action on each of your turns. If you do not spend your Standard Action to continue the channel, the spell ends. You make a Concentration Save whenever you take damage while channeling, exactly as a Sustained spell requires: roll your Focus Die against a Difficulty of 5 plus twice the spell’s tier, and on a failure the channel breaks and the spell ends. The channel also breaks immediately, ending the spell, if you become Incapacitated, are Silenced while channeling a Verbal spell, or take any action other than continuing the channel. Because a Channeled spell consumes your entire turn and ends the moment that commitment is interrupted, it is the only context in which the game’s most extreme effects, such as Sunder that can stack a Defense Rating all the way to 0, are permitted.

**SAVES**

A Save is a reactive roll made when something is happening to your character rather than something your character is initiating. When a trap springs, a spell compels you, poison takes hold, or fear claws at your mind, the GM calls for a Save. You roll the Attribute Die most relevant to what you are resisting and compare it to the Threshold set by the triggering ability or condition.

The mechanic is identical to a Task roll: meet or exceed the Threshold and you succeed. Fall short and the effect applies. The distinction between a Save and a Task is intentional and meaningful. Abilities, spells, class features, and conditions may affect one without affecting the other. Something that grants an Upgrade on Saves does not grant an Upgrade on Tasks, and vice versa, unless it explicitly states otherwise.

The four Attributes cover the full range of things a character might need to resist. Vigor Saves resist physical punishment, disease, and bodily harm. Agility Saves resist effects that require quick reactions or evasion. Focus Saves resist arcane compulsion, sensory disruption, and mental contamination. Resolve Saves resist fear, psychic assault, and attacks on the will.

A character's own failed Save does not generate Momentum for them. A Save represents resisting harm, not attempting something bold, so falling to a hostile effect is simply the effect landing. There is one important counterpart to this rule, however. When a target succeeds on a Save against a hostile spell or ability that you cast, you gain 1 Momentum Token. Your committed effort was resisted rather than wasted, and the system reflects that the same way it reflects a Glancing Blow: the attempt still feeds the party's momentum. In short, the creature making the Save never gains Momentum from it, but the caster whose effect was resisted does.

**CHALLENGE THRESHOLD**

When a character attempts a task with a meaningful chance of failure, the GM sets a Challenge Threshold. The player rolls the Attribute Die most relevant to the task. If the result meets or exceeds the Threshold, they succeed.

| **Difficulty** | **Target Number** | **Representative Task** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine | 3 | Something trained individuals can do under pressure |
| Challenging | 5 | A test that demands skill and focus |
| Demanding | 7 | The limit of what most people can reliably achieve |
| Extreme | 9 | Feats that define legendary careers |

**TASKS**

A Task is any meaningful attempt to accomplish something with uncertain outcomes outside of direct combat. Picking a lock, tracking prey across a shifting marsh, bartering with a suspicious merchant, scaling a rain-slicked cliff in the dark. When the outcome matters and failure is possible, the GM calls for a Task roll. A Task roll uses the same mechanic as any other roll: choose the Attribute Die most relevant to the attempt and roll it against the Challenge Threshold the GM has set. Meet or exceed the Threshold and the Task succeeds.

Not every action requires a Task roll. The GM should only call for one when two conditions are both true: the outcome is genuinely uncertain, and failure produces an interesting consequence. A trained physician stabilizing a stable patient does not need a roll. A physician operating in a flooded ruin by candlelight while a hostile patrol sweeps the floor above does.

**Choosing the Attribute**

The GM names the Attribute before the roll is made, but players may propose an alternative if they can justify it. Prying open a sealed vault door is Vigor by default, but a character who understands the mechanism might reasonably argue for Focus. The GM has final say. The four Attributes cover the full range of Task approaches:

- **Vigor: **Physical feats of strength, endurance, and raw force. Forcing open a sealed door, swimming against a current, carrying a wounded ally across a battlefield, resisting a disease taking hold in the body.

- **Agility: **Speed, precision, and physical dexterity. Picking a lock, moving silently through a crowded hall, scaling a cliff face, threading a needle in the dark, catching a thrown object mid-air.

- **Focus: **Intellect, perception, and arcane discipline. Deciphering a cipher, identifying a poison by its smell, navigating by dead reckoning, recalling obscure lore, tracking through difficult terrain by reading environmental signs.

- **Resolve: **Conviction, personality, and psychological strength. Convincing a reluctant informant, maintaining composure while interrogated, inspiring a demoralized group, holding position against overwhelming dread.

**Degrees of Success and Failure**

Task rolls do not produce a flat success or failure. The margin matters. The GM interprets results using the following guidance as a baseline:

- **Clean Success (meets or exceeds the Threshold): **The character accomplishes exactly what they attempted. No complications, no cost.

- **Failure by 1 or 2 (near miss): **The character falls short, but the situation does not dramatically worsen. They may try again if circumstances permit, or simply find another path. The party gains 1 Momentum Token.

- **Failure by 3 or more (hard failure): **The character fails and the situation changes in a meaningful way. An alarm is triggered, a contact grows hostile, a rope frays. The GM introduces a complication. The party gains 2 Momentum Tokens.

**Spending Momentum on Tasks**

Momentum is not locked to combat. Before any Task roll, any player may spend Momentum Tokens from the party pool, each token adding +1 to the result. This represents the group’s collective focus, preparation, and shared experience bearing on a critical moment. A character about to attempt a vital negotiation can benefit from the party’s accumulated tension just as much as a warrior can on the battlefield.

**Group Tasks**

When the entire party is attempting the same Task together, such as crossing a treacherous passage or moving silently past a patrol, the GM may call for a Group Task. Each player rolls the relevant Attribute Die. Count the results. If more than half of the rolls meet or exceed the Challenge Threshold, the group succeeds. If half or fewer succeed, the group fails and the consequence applies to everyone. Characters who failed individually may still contribute to a group success; outcomes reflect collective performance, not individual excellence.

**Assisting**

A character who is not the primary actor may attempt to assist. They describe how they are helping, roll the relevant Attribute Die, and if their result meets a Routine Threshold (3), the primary actor rolls their Task die with it Upgraded by one step. Only one character may assist on any given Task. Assistance is not always possible: the GM determines whether the nature of the Task permits it. Some things can only be done alone.

**Upgrading and Downgrading Dice**

The primary way [GAME NAME] handles difficulty and power scaling is through the Step-Die scale. Upgrading a die shifts it one step to the right (d6 becomes d8). Downgrading shifts it one step to the left. A die cannot be Upgraded beyond d12 or Downgraded below d4. Multiple Upgrades and Downgrades offset each other before resolving.

Step-Die Ceiling Overflow: If a die is already at d12 and would be Upgraded further, each excess Upgrade beyond d12 instead adds an overflow die: roll a d4 alongside the d12 and add its result. Each further Upgrade steps the overflow die up the ladder instead of adding another die (a d12 with two Upgrades rolls a d12 plus a d6; with three, a d12 plus a d8). The overflow die is a true die: it can Explode, and it benefits from properties such as Vicious exactly as the main die does. The overflow die applies only to the triggering roll and does not permanently alter the die.

**Upgrading and Downgrading Rolls**

When a roll is Upgraded, shift the Attribute Die one step up the scale before rolling. When a roll is Downgraded, shift it one step down. Multiple Upgrades and Downgrades on the same roll offset each other before resolving. A die cannot be Upgraded beyond d12 or Downgraded below d4 through this mechanism; overflow rules apply as described above.

**IMPACT DICE**

Combat in [GAME NAME] does not use to-hit rolls. You always land your strikes. The question is never whether you connect, it is whether the force behind your blow is sufficient to penetrate what stands between you and your target's body.

| **CALCULATING DAMAGE: THREE STEPS** 1. Roll your Impact Pool. This is the Attribute Die associated with your attack (Vigor for melee, Agility for ranged, Focus for spells, Resolve for authority-based abilities; weapons with the Finesse property may use Agility for melee). Tier and class features determine how many dice you roll. 2. Add the dice together. 3. Subtract the appropriate Defense Rating. The remainder is the Hit Points the target loses. Bonus Impact Dice: unless a feature explicitly states otherwise, a single Impact Roll can include a maximum of one bonus Impact Die granted by class features or resource expenditure, in addition to the dice of the attack or spell itself. When a feature allows spending multiple resource points on bonus dice, each point beyond the first Upgrades that bonus die by one step rather than adding another die. |
| --- |

**Glancing Blows**

If your total Impact is equal to or lower than the target's Defense Rating, you deal zero damage. This is a Glancing Blow. Physical contact was made, but the impact lacked sufficient force to breach the target's defenses. Some class features and weapons trigger specifically on Glancing Blows. When your attack against an enemy results in a Glancing Blow, you gain 1 Momentum Token. This is automatic, not optional: no committed strike is ever wasted, and a blow that fails to breach a defense still feeds the party's momentum.

**EXPLOSIONS AND CASCADES**

If any die in your Impact Pool rolls its maximum value, that die explodes. Roll that die again and add the result to your damage total. An attack can explode infinitely as long as each bonus roll continues to hit the die's maximum value.

**Vicious Cascades**

If a weapon or ability possesses the Vicious property, its explosions cascade upward. When a Vicious die explodes, the bonus die you roll is Upgraded by one step (d6 becomes d8, d8 becomes d10, to a maximum of d12). If that upgraded die also rolls its maximum value, it explodes and upgrades again.

**THE ACTION ECONOMY**

Combat rounds represent approximately six seconds of simultaneous action. On your turn:

- **Standard Action:** Attack, cast a major spell, use a class feature, or perform a complex task.

- **Quick Action:** Move up to your Base Speed, retrieve or stow a Readied item, or use a quick class ability.

- **Free Action:** Minor instantaneous actions, dropping an item, speaking a sentence, toggling a passive effect.

- **Reaction:** One action held in reserve to respond to a specific trigger outside your turn. Regained at the start of each of your turns.

**Readied and Stowed Items**

Readied items are immediately accessible and can be drawn or activated without significant delay: weapons holstered at the hip, a shield strapped to the arm, or a potion on a belt hook. Retrieving or stowing a Readied item is a Quick Action. Everything else is Stowed and requires a Standard Action to retrieve. A character may have a number of Readied items equal to the maximum face value of their Agility Die. See the Inventory and Carrying Capacity section for full rules.

**INITIATIVE**

At the start of combat, every participant rolls their Agility Die. The highest result acts first, with remaining participants following in descending order. Some class features or armor properties allow substituting a different Attribute Die for Initiative.

When two participants are tied, the one with the larger Agility Die acts first. A d8 beats a d6; a d10 beats a d8. If their Agility Dice are the same size, both participants reroll their Agility Dice, repeating until the tie is broken. This process repeats for every tied pair in the initiative order before play begins.

**HEALTH AND VITALITY**

Your Hit Points represent your physical integrity. Each class defines its own starting HP formula. At every level after 1st, you roll your Vigor Die and add your character level to the result to increase your maximum HP. This roll cannot explode. If the result is less than half the maximum face value of your Vigor Die, it is rounded up to that half value.

**Vitality Surge**

At Level 5 and again at Level 10, every character undergoes a Vitality Surge. In addition to the standard Vigor Die roll, your maximum HP increases by an additional amount equal to the maximum face value of your current Vigor Die.

**Death and Dying**

When reduced to 0 Hit Points, you fall Incapacitated. At the start of each of your turns, make a Vigor Save against Difficulty 5:

- **Stable:**Three successes: you stabilize. You remain unconscious but are no longer dying.

- **Death:**Three failures: you die.

- **Defying Death:**Your Vigor Save rolls its maximum face value and Explodes: you instantly regain 1 Hit Point and stand up.

**CHARACTER SCALING**

**Ability Die Increases**

At Levels 3, 5, 7, and 9, every class grants an Ability Die Increase. Permanently Upgrade one Attribute Die of your choice by one step on the Step-Die scale. Because your Defense Ratings are derived from your Attribute Dice, improving an Attribute Die simultaneously improves your offensive capability and the corresponding Defense Rating.

**Multiattack**

At Level 5, every martial class gains Multiattack, allowing two attacks with a Standard Action instead of one. Spellcasting classes receive equivalent features at Level 5 that similarly expand their offensive output. Combined Strike: when a class's Multiattack entry grants access to this rule, the character may pool both attacks against a single target into one Impact Roll, rolling the weapon dice of both attacks together and subtracting the target's Defense Rating only once. Pooled dice Explode normally, and the pooled roll remains subject to the Bonus Impact Dice rule: at most one feature-granted bonus die may join it. A Combined Strike pooled roll is a single attack and a single hit: on-hit riders, secondary effects, and Visceral or True Damage declarations apply only once, no matter how many attacks or resource points were folded into the pool. A class has access to Combined Strike only if its Multiattack entry says so.

**MOMENTUM AND TEAMWORK**

Momentum Tokens represent individual characters accumulating energy, focus, and resolve through the friction of active play. Each character maintains their own personal pool of Momentum Tokens. Tokens do not belong to the group and cannot be shared passively, but a character may choose to spend one of their own tokens to add +1 to any roll made by an ally within their immediate engagement. This is a deliberate act of support, not an automatic resource transfer. Each token is worth exactly +1 added to the final result of a roll: Impact Rolls in combat, or Task rolls outside of it. Momentum is an intentional exception to the system's preference for dice-based expression of power: momentum is small, concrete, and accumulates through persistence rather than fortune.

- **Pool Cap:**Each character may hold a maximum of 3 Momentum Tokens at any time. Tokens generated beyond this cap are lost.

- **Spending Momentum: **Spend 1 or more of your own tokens before a roll. Each token adds +1 to the final total. A character may spend their own tokens on any roll they make, or on any roll made by an ally within their immediate engagement. Multiple tokens may be spent on a single roll. Tokens must be committed before the dice are rolled. Throughout the spell lists and class features, the phrase "you gain 1 Momentum" means that specific character gains 1 token in their personal pool, subject to the individual cap.

- **Generating Momentum:** Generated by class features, GM discretion, and the following automatic triggers. A melee or ranged attack that results in a Glancing Blow generates 1 token for the attacker. A spell or ability resisted by a successful Save generates 1 token for the caster. A Task roll that fails by 1 or 2 generates 1 token for the rolling character. A Task roll that fails by 3 or more generates 2 tokens for the rolling character. Failure is never just loss; it is momentum waiting to be turned.

- **Pool Reset: **All Momentum Tokens reset to 0 upon completing a Long Rest. Any unspent tokens are lost. Momentum represents the energy and tension of active adventure; rest clears the slate.

**DAILY RESERVES**

Every character possesses a pool of consumable points for each of the four Attributes called Daily Reserves. These represent the finite well of stamina, focus, and willpower carried into each day of adventure. When an ability says 'spend 2 Focus Points' or 'spend 1 Vigor Point,' it means you remove that many points from the corresponding Reserve pool.

- **Spending: **Spent to fuel class features, spells, and tactical options. Spending is typically a Free Action, though the ability it fuels may require a Standard or Quick Action.

- **Recovery: **All expended Reserves are restored upon completing a Long Rest.

- **Capacity: **At 1st Level, each Reserve's base capacity equals the maximum face value of its starting Attribute Die, plus your class's starting bonus. At each subsequent level, total Reserve capacity increases by 4 points per your class's progression.

- **Capacity Growth:**A Reserve's maximum is its starting capacity plus all per-level gains from your class progression. There is no separate ceiling tied to the Attribute Die: the per-level gains listed in your class table are the sole determinant of capacity growth.

**MOVEMENT AND POSITIONING**

**Movement**

On your turn, use a Quick Action to move up to your Base Speed (typically 30 feet). Difficult Terrain costs twice the normal movement, 2 feet of movement for every 1 foot traveled.

**Cover**

[GAME NAME] recognizes two tiers of cover. Both require a solid object that obscures a meaningful portion of your form from your attacker. Cover bonuses apply to Vigor Defense and Agility Defense only; they have no effect against attacks targeting Focus Defense or Resolve Defense. Ignoring Cover bonuses does not bypass line of sight, however: an attack still requires a valid line of sight to its target, so a creature fully concealed with no line of sight cannot be targeted even by attacks against Focus or Resolve Defense.

- **Light Cover (+1 Vigor Defense / +1 Agility Defense):** An object obscures at least one-third of your form. Any enemy making an Impact Roll against you must Downgrade their Impact Die once.

- **Heavy Cover (+2 Vigor Defense / +2 Agility Defense):** An object obscures at least half of your form. Any enemy making an Impact Roll against you must Downgrade their entire Impact Pool. If you are fully concealed with no line of sight available, you cannot be targeted by single-target attacks.

**Opportunity Attacks**

If you move out of an enemy's melee reach without deliberately disengaging, that enemy may use their Reaction to make a single melee attack against you before you leave their reach.

**STANDARDIZED CONDITIONS**

The following conditions have consistent mechanical definitions throughout all of [GAME NAME]. When an ability applies a condition, it always means exactly what is written here unless the source explicitly states otherwise.

**Condition Stacking Rules**

Conditions from different sources stack freely in terms of their simultaneous effects. A creature can be Restrained, Dazed, and Blinded at the same time with no cap on how many distinct conditions apply. However, identical conditions from multiple sources do not compound. If two sources both apply the Dazed condition, the target is Dazed once; the second application is redundant, not additive. The one explicit exception is Sundered, which stacks deliberately: each application reduces the specified Defense Rating by 1 further, with a floor of 0. All other conditions are binary, present or absent.

- **Dazed:** Cannot take Reactions. The Impact Die for their next attack is Downgraded.

- **Sundered:** One specific Defense Rating (Vigor, Agility, Focus, or Resolve Defense) is reduced by 1. The inflicting ability specifies which. When stated as lasting until the end of the inflictor's next turn, the rating is restored at that time. When stated as permanent, the reduction persists for the remainder of combat. No Defense Rating can be reduced below 0.

- **Bleeding:** Takes 2 True Damage at the start of each of their turns until they or an ally spends a Standard Action to treat the wound, or until they receive magical healing. Bleeding never stacks as separate instances; class features may escalate an existing Bleed by stated flat amounts, to a maximum of 6 True Damage per turn.

- **Restrained:** Speed is reduced to 0. All Impact Rolls made against this target are Upgraded. The Restrained condition does not prevent teleportation or planar movement, a Restrained creature may still use abilities that move them instantaneously if those abilities do not require spending movement. Effects that specifically prevent teleportation must state so explicitly.

- **Prone:** The creature has been knocked to the ground. A Prone creature's Speed is 0 until they spend a Quick Action to stand. Impact Rolls made against a Prone creature from within 15ft are Upgraded. Impact Rolls made against a Prone creature from more than 15ft away are Downgraded. A Prone creature may still take actions while on the ground.

- **Exhausted:** All four Attribute Dice are Downgraded.

- **Incapacitated:** Cannot take any actions, move, or speak. Considered an object for targeting purposes.

- **Invisible:** Cannot be seen by ordinary means. Impact Rolls against this target are Downgraded. The Invisible character's first attack before detection is Upgraded.

- **Hidden:** The creature's location is unknown to its enemies. A Hidden creature cannot be targeted by single-target attacks; area-of-effect attacks affect it normally. Attacking, casting a spell, or otherwise revealing yourself ends the Hidden status unless a feature states otherwise. An enemy may spend a Quick Action and succeed on a Focus Task against Difficulty 5 to locate a Hidden creature; if they do, the creature is no longer Hidden from that enemy, but Impact Rolls that enemy makes against it are Downgraded until the creature is fully revealed. Becoming Hidden requires a class feature or a position that fully breaks line of sight.

- **Grappled:** The creature is physically held. Its Speed is 0 and it cannot benefit from any effect that would increase its Speed. The Grappled creature may still take actions, but the grappling effect may impose further restrictions as stated by its source. Unless the source states otherwise, a Grappled creature may spend a Standard Action to attempt a Vigor Save against Difficulty 5 to break free, and the condition ends if the grappler is Incapacitated or moves out of reach.

- **Blinded:** Cannot see. All Impact Rolls they make are Downgraded. Cannot benefit from Cover.

- **Silenced:** Cannot speak or produce sound. Cannot cast spells with a Verbal component. All Resolve-based Tasks involving speech or persuasion automatically fail.

- **Slowed:** Base Speed is reduced by a stated amount (a fixed number of feet). A Slowed creature cannot benefit from any effect that would increase its Speed while Slowed. Unlike Restrained, a Slowed creature can still move; it simply moves less.

- **Frightened:** The creature Downgrades all Impact Dice on attacks made against the source of its fear, and it cannot willingly move closer to that source. It may otherwise act normally.

- **Concealed:** The creature is partially hidden from sight or sense. Impact Rolls against a Concealed creature are Downgraded. A fully Concealed creature with no available line of sight cannot be targeted by single-target attacks, identical to full Cover, though Concealment may come from sources other than a solid object.

- **Marked:** The creature has been designated by a single source. Allies of the source gain a stated benefit when attacking the Marked creature, defined by the marking ability. A creature can carry only one mark per source at a time; a new mark from the same source replaces the previous one. Marking is a designation, not a contest, and is not resisted by a Save.

- **Burning:** Takes a stated amount of Fire damage at the start of each of its turns for a stated duration, or until the creature or an ally spends a Standard Action to extinguish it, or it receives any amount of magical healing. Burning from a single source does not stack with itself; reapplying it refreshes the duration.

**The Decay Condition (Entropy School)**

Decay is a stacking condition unique to the Entropy school of magic. It represents the progressive deterioration of a target's physical and magical defenses as void energy corrodes their structure. Decay has four stages. Each time an Entropy attack successfully hits an already-Decaying target, Decay advances by one stage. Decay does not advance on a Glancing Blow. Certain Entropy spells and abilities may advance Decay by additional stages or at accelerated rates as specified in their descriptions.

- **Decay Stage 1:** The target's Vigor Defense is Sundered by 1.

- **Decay Stage 2:** The target's Vigor Defense and Agility Defense are each Sundered by 1 additionally.

- **Decay Stage 3:** The target's Vigor Defense, Agility Defense, and Focus Defense are each Sundered by 1 additionally.

- **Decay Stage 4:** All four of the target's Defense Ratings are each Sundered by 1 additionally.

These Sunder values are cumulative across all four stages. A target at Stage 4 has their Vigor Defense Sundered by 4 total, Agility Defense Sundered by 3 total, Focus Defense Sundered by 2 total, and Resolve Defense Sundered by 1 total, all subject to the standard floor of 0. Decay is a lingering corruption. It persists until cleansed by a Long Rest. It does not expire at the end of combat.

**The Petrification Condition (Transmutation School)**

Petrification is a staged condition unique to the Transmutation school, representing a body slowly turning to inert stone. It advances in three stages. At the start of each affected turn, the target makes a Vigor Save against Difficulty 7; a failure advances Petrification one stage and a success holds it at its current stage. Petrification is an escalating hazard the target can always act against, never an instant disable.

- **Petrification Stage 1:** The target's Base Speed is halved, and Impact Dice that rely on precision against it are Downgraded.

- **Petrification Stage 2:** The target's Speed drops to 0 and it loses its Reaction. It may still take its Standard and Quick Actions and attack normally.

- **Petrification Stage 3:** The target is Restrained and treated as partly stone. All damage dealt to it becomes True Damage, Impact Rolls against it are Upgraded, and all of its Defense Ratings except Vigor Defense drop to 0. The target may still take actions, but it cannot move. Removing stages of Petrification requires anti-transmutation magic.

**Resonant Links, Compulsion, and Domination (Resonance School)**

These three mechanics are unique to the Resonance school and are referenced throughout its spells.

A **Resonant Link** forms when two or more creatures take Psychic damage from the same Resonance spell in the same round, or when a spell explicitly establishes one. Linked creatures remain connected until one of them reaches 0 Hit Points or combat ends. Spells that exploit Links grow stronger as more Links accumulate on the battlefield.

**Compulsion** forces a specific action onto a target on the target's own turn. A compelled target performs the forced action and then acts freely with the remainder of its turn. Compulsion never removes a target's turn; it directs one part of it.

**Domination** replaces one of a target's actions with a command inserted by the caster. A dominated target keeps its own movement, retains emotional response and basic survival instinct, and will never knowingly walk into certain death. As with Compulsion, Domination directs a target rather than disabling it. No Resonance effect ever removes a creature's whole turn.

**EQUIPMENT: WEAPON CONFIGURATIONS**

All weapons in [GAME NAME] are categorized as either Light or Heavy. A weapon’s category determines what configurations are available to the character wielding it. The mechanical identity of any weapon comes from two sources: its weight category, which governs configuration rules, and its Explosion Rider, which governs what happens at the moment of maximum impact.

**Weapon Weight**

**Light.** Can be wielded one-handed. Compatible with shield, free hand, and dual wielding configurations.

**Heavy.** Requires two hands and a Vigor Die of d8 or higher. Adds one additional die to your Impact Pool once per turn, on the first attack you make each turn. Cannot be dual wielded without an explicit class feature permitting it.

**Finesse.** A weapon with the Finesse property may use your Agility Die in place of your Vigor Die for its Impact Pool. All other melee rules apply normally. Weapons list Finesse among their properties; it has no Attribute Die requirement.

**Dual Wielding**

You may dual wield two Light weapons simultaneously. When your primary weapon’s Impact Die triggers an Explosion, your offhand weapon automatically strikes the same target as a separate roll. That secondary strike can itself Explode normally and applies its own Explosion Rider independently. Heavy weapons cannot be dual wielded without an explicit class feature permitting it.

**Free Hand**

When you wield a one-handed weapon with no shield, focus, or offhand weapon occupying your other hand, you have a Free Hand. Your Reaction may be used to perform any of the following in addition to its normal uses.

**Grab.** Trigger: an enemy within your melee reach attacks you and the result is a Glancing Blow. You impose the Restrained condition on that enemy until the start of their next turn. You can only Grab a creature no more than one size category larger than you. On their turn, they may attempt a Vigor Save against Difficulty 5 as a Free Action to end the condition immediately. They are not required to attempt this Save.

**Redirect.** Trigger: an enemy voluntarily moves through your melee reach without disengaging. You force them to end their movement at the nearest point of your reach. This does not trigger an Opportunity Attack.

**Parry.** Trigger: an enemy within your melee reach hits you with a melee attack. Downgrade that enemy’s Impact Pool by one step for that hit. Parry cannot be used against ranged attacks, spells, or area effects.

**Casting and Martial Reactions.** If you cast any spell on your turn, you may not use Grab, Redirect, or Parry for the remainder of that round. A character commits either to spellcasting or to those martial reactions in a given round, not both. This is a deliberate balance limit on hybrid fighters and is independent of what your hands are holding.

**Shield Block**

When you have a shield equipped, your Reaction may be used to perform the following in addition to its normal uses.

**Intercept.** Trigger: an enemy’s Impact Die explodes against you. You set the result of that Explosion die to 1, preventing it from Exploding further. If multiple dice in the same Impact Pool Explode simultaneously, you may only Intercept one of them. Your Reaction is spent regardless of how many Explosions remain.

**Unarmed Combat**

Unarmed strikes use your Vigor Die as the Impact Die. Unarmed strikes have one Rider.

**Grapple.** When your Impact Die Explodes, you may either roll the bonus Explosion die as normal or impose the Restrained condition on the target until the start of your next turn. You cannot do both. This choice is made at the moment of Explosion.

**Spellcasting Components**

Component types describe how a spell is performed. They are primarily flavor and impose no hand requirements: a caster with empty hands can cast any spell they know. Components matter in only two situations, described below.

**Verbal.** The spell requires speech. A character who is Silenced, gagged, or otherwise unable to produce sound cannot cast Verbal spells. This is the one hard component restriction.

**Somatic.** The spell requires gesture. Somatic spells can be cast with a free hand, while holding an Arcane Armament, or while holding a weapon, since a caster's movements are not so precise as to be blocked by a full hand. Somatic components impose no mechanical cost on their own.

**Material.** The spell consumes or focuses through physical matter. A Material spell requires that you either hold a spellcasting focus or an Arcane Armament, or have the specific material a spell names. A caster with truly empty hands and no focus cannot cast Material spells; any Armament satisfies this requirement.

**Class Weapon Restrictions**

Each class lists the weapon configurations available to it. A character may wield any weapon outside their class restrictions, but doing so carries two costs: their Impact Pool is Downgraded, and the weapon’s Explosion Rider does not apply. Class features may explicitly expand a character’s available weapon configurations. Such expansions are permanent and override the base restriction for that configuration.

**Arcane Armaments**

An Arcane Armament is a caster's equivalent of a weapon: an implement that channels magic and grants an Explosion Rider to the spells cast through it. Just as a weapon's Rider fires when a martial's Impact die explodes, an Armament's Rider fires when a spell's Impact die explodes (rolls its maximum face). A spell cast with no Armament in hand still functions normally; it simply gains no Rider. Casting a Glancing Blow never triggers a Rider, only an Explosion does.

Armaments carry the Conduit tag, which is the property that allows a spell's exploding die to inherit the Armament's Rider. An implement without the Conduit tag is a cosmetic focus only. As with weapons, if a character casts through an Armament their class does not grant access to, the Armament's Rider does not apply.

Armaments are balanced as peers. None is mechanically stronger for occupying more hands, and no Armament adds a die to your Impact Pool; the bonus die remains exclusive to Heavy weapons. Whether an Armament is worn or held is a matter of logistics (a held Armament occupies a hand that could otherwise carry a weapon or shield), not of power. A caster chooses an Armament for the Rider that fits their style, not for a hand-count advantage. The arcane Staff described here is a distinct object from the martial Quarterstaff in the weapon tables; one channels spells, the other is swung.

The standard Arcane Armaments and their Explosion Riders:

- **Wand** (held, one hand). Rider, Pierce: the bonus damage from the exploding die is dealt as True Damage.

- **Rod** (held, one hand). Rider, Punish: the Defense Rating this spell targeted is Sundered by 1 until the end of your next turn.

- **Orb** (held or worn). Rider, Resonate: you generate 1 additional Momentum Token.

- **Relic** (worn). Rider, Echo: one other enemy within 10ft of the target takes damage of the spell's type equal to the Explosion die.

- **Holy Symbol** (worn). Rider, Sanctify: you or an ally within 30ft regains Hit Points equal to the Explosion die.

- **Totem** (held or worn). Rider, Spirit Surge: the next ally to act before your next turn Upgrades their first Impact Die.

- **Staff** (held, two hands). Rider, Cascade: the bonus Explosion die is Upgraded one step, and if it too rolls its maximum it explodes and Upgrades again.

- **Spell Book** (held). Rider, Compound: you may immediately apply one Tier 0 spell you know to the same target as a Free effect.

**EQUIPMENT: ARMOR CATEGORIES**

Armor in [GAME NAME] falls into four categories. Three are physical, Light, Medium, and Heavy, and one is magical. Your class proficiencies determine which categories you can wear without penalty. Wearing armor you are not proficient with forces you to Downgrade all Attribute Dice while equipped.

| **Category** | **Defense Bonus** | **Who Wears It** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light Armor | +1 to +2 Vigor Points / Agility Defense | Agile classes, scouts, assassins | No Speed penalty. Stealth checks unaffected. |
| Medium Armor | +2 to +3 Vigor Points / Agility Defense | Warriors, rangers, hybrid classes | May impose Stealth penalties depending on type. |
| Heavy Armor | +3 to +5 Vigor Points / Agility Defense | Frontline warriors, defenders | Requires a Vigor Die of d8 or higher. Speed penalty of −5ft. Stealth checks Downgraded or failed. |
| Arcane Vestments | +1 to +3 Focus Points / Resolve Defense | Spellcasting classes | Grants defense against magical and mental attacks rather than physical ones. No Speed penalty. Cannot be combined with any physical armor category. |

Arcane Vestments are robes, cloaks, woven garments, and ritual wear imbued with magical warding. They do not protect against swords or arrows, they protect against spells, psychic attacks, and the corruption of the world, granting bonuses to both Focus Defense and Resolve Defense. Armor categories cannot be combined. A character wears one category at a time.

**CONJURED OBJECTS AND STRUCTURES**

Some spells and class features conjure physical objects, barriers, walls, and structures into existence. These are real, tangible, and can be destroyed by enemies. Unless a spell specifies otherwise, all conjured objects follow these rules.

**Defense Ratings of Conjured Objects**

Conjured objects have a Vigor Defense equal to half the maximum face value of the caster's current Focus Die, following the same baseline formula as character defenses. They have an Agility Defense of 0, elemental damage passes through conjured matter without reduction. They have a Focus Defense of 0 and a Resolve Defense of 0. Conjured objects cannot be targeted by effects that require a living creature.

**Hit Points and Destruction**

Each conjured object has a specific HP value defined by the spell that created it. When a conjured object is reduced to 0 HP, it is instantly destroyed and vanishes completely unless the spell specifies that debris or residue remains. Area-of-effect spells and attacks that overlap with a conjured object deal their full damage to it. A single powerful strike from a Tier 2 or Tier 3 Evocation spell can bring down most Tier 1 conjured barriers in a single round, conjured terrain is tactically meaningful but not permanent or indestructible.

**Targeting Conjured Objects**

Any creature may target a conjured object with an attack or spell, provided they have line of sight to it. Attacks targeting a conjured object always use the object's Vigor Defense as the relevant defense, regardless of the damage type of the attack.

**ILLUSION AND BELIEF**

Illusion spells create false sensory information that creatures experience as real. An illusion is believed until a creature has concrete cause to doubt it. The GM determines when a disbelief opportunity arises based on the following triggers.

**Disbelief Triggers**

- **Physical Contact:** A creature physically passes through an illusion or an illusory entity.

- **Zero Damage:** A creature takes zero damage from an attack made by an illusory entity.

- **Active Scrutiny:** A creature spends a Quick Action to actively scrutinize the illusion and succeeds on a Focus Save where the Difficulty equals the caster's Focus Defense. This is not an opposed roll; the caster's Focus Defense serves as the Target Number.

**Effect of Disbelief**

A creature that successfully disbelieves an illusion sees through it entirely. The illusion ceases to affect that creature's behavior. However, disbelief is a personal realization; it does not automatically communicate itself to allies. A creature that disbelieves may spend a Quick Action to warn one ally, granting that ally an immediate scrutiny attempt without needing to spend their own Quick Action.

**Sensory Range of Illusions**

Not all illusions affect every sense equally. Tier 0 and Tier 1 Concealment and Fabrication spells affect sight only unless the spell explicitly states otherwise. Tier 2 and higher Illusion spells affect all senses including smell, sound, touch, and magical detection unless the spell explicitly states a limitation. Individual spells will note their sensory range in their effect text.

**Compelled Movement and Prone**

Some Illusion spells compel a creature to move in a specific direction. If a creature subject to compelled movement is currently Prone, they must first spend a Quick Action to stand before the compelled movement executes. If they choose not to stand or cannot do so, the compelled movement is lost but all other effects of the spell still apply normally. The Prone condition does not grant immunity to compulsion; it simply requires the standard cost to stand before movement can occur.

Phantasm spells are the only Illusion spells that deal damage. Their damage is Psychic and targets Resolve Defense, representing the psychological shock of experiencing a terrifying false reality. If a creature successfully disbelieves a Phantasm spell before its next damage tick, the ongoing damage stops immediately as the psychological mechanism that made the harm real is removed.

**REST AND RECOVERY**

**Short Rest**

Requires 30 minutes of relative calm. Roll your Vigor Die and regain that many Hit Points. Can be done any number of times provided a Healer's Kit or bandages are available. Does not restore Daily Reserves.

**Long Rest**

Requires 6 hours of sleep and 2 hours of light activity. Restores all lost Hit Points and all spent Daily Reserves.

**RANGE**

Melee reach is 5 feet for all characters unless modified by size, a weapon property, or a class feature. Range for all other attacks, spells, and abilities is defined in the entry for that weapon, spell, or ability. There are no universal range increment penalties in this system; each source specifies its own effective range and any rules that apply beyond it.

**LIGHT AND DARKNESS**

Visibility operates on two states: lit and dark. Any light source, regardless of size, fully negates darkness within its radius as defined by that source. There is no intermediate dim light state.

In darkness, all sight-based Task and Save rolls are Downgraded. Impact Rolls made against targets in darkness are also Downgraded. These penalties apply whether you are in darkness yourself or your target is. A character who cannot see their target suffers the full penalty regardless of their own lighting conditions.

Darkvision, the ability to see normally in darkness, is rare. It is not a standard trait of any origin and is only available through specific class features, spells, or items. When a character with Darkvision is in darkness, they ignore all darkness penalties entirely.

**FALLING DAMAGE**

Falls of 10 feet or less deal no damage. For every 5 feet fallen beyond the initial 10, the falling creature takes 1d4 True Damage. This damage bypasses all Defense Ratings. A creature falling 20 feet takes 2d4 True Damage. A creature falling 30 feet takes 4d4 True Damage, and so on. There is no maximum cap; falling from great heights is lethal.

Falling damage dice do not explode. A creature that is already Incapacitated when it falls takes the damage normally and the result is applied to their death track immediately.

**HEALING**

When a character receives healing, they regain Hit Points equal to the amount specified by the source. Healing cannot raise a character above their maximum Hit Points. Excess healing is lost.

A character at 0 Hit Points who receives any healing immediately regains that many Hit Points and is no longer Incapacitated. They stand up, their death track resets, and they may act normally on their next turn. Healing does not require the character to have stabilized first. Any healing applied to an Incapacitated character brings them back.

**INVENTORY AND CARRYING CAPACITY**

Every character has two categories of inventory: Readied and Stowed. Readied items are immediately accessible. Stowed items are packed away and require more effort to retrieve.

- **Readied Slots: **A number of slots equal to the maximum face value of your Agility Die. Items in Readied slots are holstered, strapped, or otherwise immediately accessible. Retrieving or stowing a Readied item is a Quick Action.

- **Stowed Inventory: **Everything else a character carries that is not Readied. Retrieving a Stowed item requires a Standard Action. There is no hard cap on Stowed inventory, but the GM should apply common sense to what a character can reasonably carry. Size affects Stowed capacity as defined in the Size Categories section.

**SIZE CATEGORIES**

Every creature and object in the game occupies one of six size categories. Size affects melee reach, Vigor Defense, and carrying capacity. Most player characters are Medium. Size is defined by a creature’s stat block or by the GM for improvised objects.

- **Tiny: **Insects, rats, small birds. No melee reach beyond their own space. Vigor Defense -2. Stowed capacity: 0.5x Vigor Die maximum. Single-target melee attacks against a Tiny creature Downgrade the attacker's entire Impact Pool; it is simply hard to land a solid blow on something so small. Area of effect attacks affect them normally. Automatically fail any Vigor Task requiring physical mass or force.

- **Small: **Halflings, gnomes, cats. Melee reach 5ft. Vigor Defense -1. Stowed capacity: 0.75x Vigor Die maximum (round down).

- **Medium: **Humans, elves, dwarves. Melee reach 5ft. No Vigor Defense modifier. Stowed capacity: 1x Vigor Die maximum.

- **Large: **Ogres, horses, bears. Melee reach 10ft. Vigor Defense +1. Stowed capacity: 2x Vigor Die maximum.

- **Huge: **Giants, young dragons. Melee reach 15ft. Vigor Defense +3. Stowed capacity: 3x Vigor Die maximum.

- **Colossal: **Krakens, ancient dragons. Melee reach 20ft. Vigor Defense +6. Stowed capacity: 4x Vigor Die maximum. Any creature three or more size categories smaller than a Colossal creature treats it as terrain for the purposes of movement and positioning, and cannot move it through any means that would require moving a creature.

The terrain rule applies to any size pairing with a gap of three or more categories, not only Colossal creatures. A Huge creature is terrain to a Tiny one. A Large creature is terrain to nothing by this rule alone. The GM determines what actions are possible when creatures interact across extreme size differences, with positional advantages and creative approaches left to the table rather than defined by hard rules.

**LEVELING AND MILESTONES**

Characters advance through ten levels. Progression is milestone-based: the GM determines when the party levels up based on story progress, significant accomplishments, and the natural arc of the campaign. There is no experience point tracking. A level-up happens when it feels earned.

Each level grants class-specific features and improvements. The universal progression elements defined in this chapter apply to every class: Hit Point increases at each level after 1st, Ability Die Increases at Levels 3, 5, 7, and 9, Vitality Surges at Levels 5 and 10, and Multiattack or equivalent features at Level 5. Full detail on what each level grants is found in the character creation and class chapters.

The GM should consider leveling the party together. Individual advancement creates mechanical imbalance and can undermine group cohesion. Unless a campaign specifically calls for it, all players level up at the same time.
