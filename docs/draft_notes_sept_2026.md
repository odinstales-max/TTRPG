## Section 1 — Potential System Changes Being Considered

**Design intent:**
Attempt to have various options when building/playing a character.

**Attributes:**
- Vigor
- Agility
- Focus
- Resolve

**Attribute Point System:**
Spend points for abilities.

**Abilities:**
Cause buffs, debuffs, increased damage, increased healing, status effects.

**Actions — Action Point System (proposed replacement for the current
Standard/Quick/Free/Reaction structure):**
3 action points, usable for:
- Attacks
- Defends
- Maneuvers
- Interactions

Every action costs a flat **1 AP** — characters get 3 things per turn,
full stop. Reactions sit outside the 3 AP pool as a separate resource, as
they do in the current system.

Cost differentiation between a basic attack and a stronger ability does
**not** come from AP under this system — it comes from Reserve
(resource-point) expenditure instead:
- **Basic attacks** are free of any resource cost and roll only 1
  Attribute Die.
- **Featured attacks** (spells, abilities) cost Reserve points, scaled to
  their strength — buying additional Attribute Dice rolled and/or an
  added effect.
- Since a character's Reserve pool is level-limited, combat becomes a
  resource-management layer sitting on top of the flat 3-AP turn
  structure: the AP economy governs *how many things* you do, Reserve
  spend governs *how strong* those things are.

> **Open conflict — Multiattack:** Multiattack currently grants martial
> classes 2 attacks from a single Standard Action. Under a flat 1-AP-per-
> attack economy, any character can already spend 2 of their 3 AP on two
> separate basic attacks with no feature required — so Multiattack as
> written stops doing anything. Needs a redesign.
>
> **Direction chosen: Option D — Reserve-based discount.** Multiattack
> reduces the Reserve cost of a second attack-adjacent ability used that
> turn, rather than touching AP at all. This keeps AP purely about tempo
> and pushes Multiattack's power into the Reserve layer, consistent with
> how the rest of combat's power scaling now works. Tradeoff to keep in
> mind: it does nothing on a turn where the martial character is only
> throwing basic attacks, since those already cost 0 Reserve — it only
> matters on turns they're also spending Reserve on something else.
> Exact discount amount (flat reduction vs. percentage, which
> abilities qualify as "attack-adjacent") still needs figuring out.
>
> **Also under consideration, as a future Talent rather than a baseline
> class feature: Option C** — pooling 2 attacks into one Impact Roll
> (Combined-Strike-style) for bonus dice or an added effect, spending the
> same AP as 2 separate attacks. Left as a later Talent option rather than
> baseline Multiattack.

**Spellcasting** draws from the same pool:
- Attacks
- Defends
- Maneuvers
- Interactions
- Spellcasting

**Step-Die System:**
Attributes start at d4 and improve by natural progression (leveling) to a
max of **d10**. The existing Ability Die Increase levels (3/5/7/9) remain
the only path to d10 — nothing else raises an Attribute Die that far.
Reaching **d12** is not achievable through natural progression at all; it
would require other features/abilities (e.g., an Upgrade-granting Talent,
item, or class feature) to push a d10 Attribute one step further. *(Not
being decided right now — noted for later.)*

**Talents — Level Placement:**
Talents are gained on even levels: **2, 4, 6, 8, 10.**

**Exploding Dice:**
When a die result rolls max, the subsequent die rerolls and adds to the total.

> **Design implication:** Talents (even levels) and Ability Die Increases
> (odd levels 3/5/7/9) now interleave — a character alternates between
> gaining a Talent and stepping up an Attribute Die almost every level.
> That's a clean, predictable rhythm for players. One thing to square
> away later: whether a d12 Upgrade (whenever you decide how it's granted)
> would come from a specific Talent choice at one of those even levels, or
> from an entirely separate source outside the Talent track.

## Section 2 — Talents & Traits Notes

**Talents**
- Defined as features that require a minimum character level.
- Always beneficial — a physical improvement to the character.
- Combat- and class-oriented (the "feat" analog).
- *(Acknowledged exception: some Talents may still affect non-combat
  situations.)*

**Traits**
- Features locked behind an Attribute Die threshold, split into two
  flavors: **Social** and **Good/Bad**.
- Physical or personality-defining changes — can cut either way (a
  strength and a corresponding weakness), e.g.:
  - Quick-witted vs. dim-witted
  - Perceptive vs. near-sighted
  - Agile vs. hobble-footed
- More about defining a character's personality and social interactions
  (the "background feature" analog).
- *(Acknowledged exception: some Traits may still affect combat.)*

**Readied Utility Belt**
- A number of Readied (utility belt) items equal to the max face value of
  your Agility Die.
- Stowed items are all other accessible items during combat.
- All other items cannot be obtained quickly.

## Talents vs. Traits — Reconciled Definition

| | **Talents** | **Traits** |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | Minimum character level | Attribute Die threshold |
| Valence | Always positive | Can be a strength *or* a weakness (paired) |
| Focus | Combat / class-oriented | Personality / social / physical-defining |
| Analogy | Feat | Background feature |
| Exceptions | Some affect non-combat | Some affect combat |

This gives you two genuinely distinct unlock tracks rather than one
undifferentiated "ability tree" — Talents as the power-progression axis,
Traits as the characterization axis with built-in trade-offs. Worth
deciding whether Traits are ever *chosen* (pick your strength, accept the
paired weakness) or something closer to background/origin-determined and
fixed at character creation.
